- **RabbitMQ hooks for Node.js: `trailbeam.amqplib`** (QV-310, part 6 of 6 of RabbitMQ support; contract 4.0,
  `contract/span-contract-amqp.md`). `npm install trailbeam amqplib`, then `trailbeam.amqplib.install()` once per
  process. The same spans as the Python pika hooks, for amqplib's promise and callback APIs (amqplib 0.10 or later,
  an optional peer dependency loaded only by `install`):
  - `trailbeam publish` for every `publish` and `sendToQueue`, ending at the broker's confirm on a confirm channel,
    failed on a nack or when the channel closes first (with the reply-code name, such as `NOT_FOUND`);
  - `trailbeam return` for an unroutable `mandatory` publish;
  - `trailbeam deliver` for consumer deliveries (before the application's callback) and `get`, with the receive count
    from `x-acquired-count` or `x-delivery-count`, and the dead-letter move, its reason and time from `x-death`;
  - `trailbeam ack`, `nack` and `reject` (also `ackAll` and `nackAll`), one per delivery covered;
  - `trailbeam cancel` for client and broker cancels, and `trailbeam channel_closed` for each delivery still open when
    its channel or connection closes or is lost, with who closed it and the broker's reply text.
  - The hooks make no broker calls of their own; confirm callbacks, `waitForConfirms` and `return` listeners work as
    before. Tenant from a message header, opt-in body capture of text bodies, and an opt-in `setMessageId`
    (`TRAILBEAM_AMQP_SET_MESSAGE_ID`) for publishes without a `messageId`.
- **CI tests the amqplib hooks against `rabbitmq:4`**, on the newest amqplib and on 0.10.9, and passes their spans
  through the server's ingest and timelines. amqplib 0.10.5 and older can't connect to RabbitMQ 4.1 or later at all
  (the broker refuses their `frame_max` of 4096).
