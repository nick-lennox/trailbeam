- **RabbitMQ hooks for Python: `trailbeam.pika`** (QV-309, part 5 of 6 of RabbitMQ support; contract 4.0,
  `contract/span-contract-amqp.md`). `pip install "trailbeam[pika]"`, then `trailbeam.pika.install()` once per process.
  The hooks sit on `pika.channel.Channel`, so every pika connection type is covered (pika 1.3 or later), and emit:
  - `trailbeam publish` for every `basic_publish`, ending at the broker's confirm in confirm mode, failed on a nack or
    when the channel closes first (with the reply-code name, such as `NOT_FOUND`);
  - `trailbeam return` for an unroutable `mandatory` publish, also when pika raises `UnroutableError`;
  - `trailbeam deliver` for consumer deliveries (before the application's callback) and `basic_get`, with the receive
    count from `x-acquired-count` or `x-delivery-count`, and the dead-letter move, its reason and time from `x-death`;
  - `trailbeam ack`, `nack` and `reject`, one per delivery covered;
  - `trailbeam cancel` for client and broker cancels, and `trailbeam channel_closed` for each delivery still open when
    its channel or connection closes or is lost, with who closed it and the broker's reply text.
  - The hooks make no broker calls of their own. Tenant from a message header, opt-in body capture of text bodies,
    and an opt-in `set_message_id` (`TRAILBEAM_AMQP_SET_MESSAGE_ID`) for publishes without a `message_id`.
- **The demo runs RabbitMQ too**: `docker compose -f demo/compose.yaml up` adds a RabbitMQ 4 broker with a Python
  publisher and consumer whose messages are acked, requeued, redelivered after a closed connection, and dead-lettered.
- **CI tests the pika hooks against `rabbitmq:4`**, on the newest pika and on 1.3.2.
